7. STABILIZER EXCLUSIONS

We use the following elementary lemma. Suppose h fixes the nonrational
part of the reduction of D, and C is the rational cusp lift of the
remaining part, of degree c. If (h-1)J(Q) is finite, then
 y=[h(D-C)-(D-C)] is rational torsion reducing to zero, so2y=0.
If y=0 and gon_Q(X)>15+c, or merely2y=0 and gon_Q(X)>2*(15+c),
there can be no nonconstant rational function with that divisor.
Consequently hD=D and hC=C. Diamonds act freely on rational cusps,
so the order of h must divide c.

For an odd-order h acting trivially on J(Q)[2], the norm relation
(1+h+...+h^(r-1))y=0 gives y=0. The actual JOne homology certificates
show J_1(53)(Q)[2]=0, and diamond2 acts trivially on J_1(73)(Q)[2].
Their integral lattices are those of JOne, not an isogenous substitute.

8.2. DESCENT TO AN IRREDUCIBLE CUBIC DIVISOR ON Z

Suppose such a D exists. Choose a rational cusp c on X, and put
 h=delta_3. Then
 [hD-D]=(h-1)[D-15c]+15[hc-c]
is rational torsion, by (i) and Manin--Drinfeld. It reduces to zero,
by (iv). Hence [hD-D]=0. A nonzero principal divisor hD-D would give
a rational function over Q of degree at most15. This is impossible,
since gon_Q(X)>=325*(61^2-1)/65536>15. Thus hD=D as divisors.

The order5 group H5 acts freely on characteristic-zero noncuspidal
points of X: a diamond stabilizer there can only have order dividing
2 or3, from the extra elliptic automorphisms. Therefore D is the
pullback of an irreducible cubic divisor D_Y on Y. Its image D_Z on Z
has degree1 or3. Degree1 would give a rational noncuspidal point on
X_0(61), forbidden by Mazur's rational prime-isogeny theorem. Thus
D_Z is irreducible of degree3, and f_*D=5D_Z.

Reduction commutes with f and the diamonds. Since delta_2 fixes P15,
we obtain 5*(s red(D_Z)-red(D_Z))=0 in the divisor group of the special
fibre of Z. That group is torsion-free, so red(D_Z) is s-invariant.
(The transitive H15 action above also gives red(D_Z)=3S for one
rational point S.) Choose a rational cusp c_Z of Z. Then
 [sD_Z-D_Z]=(s-1)[D_Z-3c_Z]+3[sc_Z-c_Z]
is rational torsion by (ii) and Manin--Drinfeld on the quotient.
It reduces to zero, hence [sD_Z-D_Z]=0 by injectivity of torsion
reduction on J_Z.

8.3. THE RATIONAL GONALITY OF Z IS AT LEAST4

First C=X_0(61) has no Q-map to P^1 of degree at most3. An exact
smooth canonical model, returned by Magma's SmallModularCurve(61),
is a complete intersection of degrees2 and3 in P^3. Its quadric is
 Q=x2^2-x1*x3+x1*x4+x3*x4.
Its symmetric Gram matrix has rank4 and determinant -1/4. Adjunction
identifies O(1) with the canonical sheaf, so C is nonhyperelliptic.
A rational degree3 pencil would give a rational ruling of this quadric:
the canonical spans of the general fibres are lines contained in Q.
But the nonsquare discriminant means the two rulings are exchanged
over Q(i), and neither is defined over Q. This excludes a Q-map of
degree3. Genus4 excludes degree1. This also agrees with the published
classification: Hasegawa--Shimura, Trigonal modular curves, and
Jeon, Modular curves with infinitely many cubic points, Theorem2.3.
The latter is available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2020.09.006 .
Here rational gonality is essential; C is trigonal over Q(i).

Suppose Z had a Q-map u:Z -> P^1 of degree d=2 or3. Since g(Z)=8
exceeds (d-1)^2, its degree-d pencil is unique, by the
Castelnuovo--Severi inequality. For these prime degrees, two distinct
pencils give a birational map to a bidegree(d,d) curve in P^1 x P^1,
which would have genus at most(d-1)^2.

Thus u s=alpha u for alpha in PGL_2(Q), with alpha^2=1. If alpha=1,
u factors through C. This is impossible for d=3 by parity, and for
d=2 because it would give a degree1 map C -> P^1. If alpha is a
nontrivial involution, pass to quotients to obtain a Q-map
C -> P^1/<alpha> of degree d. The quotient of P^1 is rational over Q
(for example by Luroth's theorem), so this contradicts the preceding
paragraph. Degree1 is excluded by genus. Therefore gon_Q(Z)>=4.

8.4. THE CONTRADICTION

The principal divisor sD_Z-D_Z has positive part of degree at most3.
The gonality bound from Section8.3 forces it to be zero. Thus sD_Z=D_Z.
The three distinct geometric points of D_Z form one Galois orbit, and
s commutes with Galois. All its orbits on those three points have the
same length. Since the length divides2 and must divide3, it is1.
All three points are fixed by s.

Riemann--Hurwitz for the double cover Z -> C gives exactly
       (2*g(Z)-2)-2*(2*g(C)-2)=14-12=2
geometric fixed points of s in characteristic zero. Three fixed points
are impossible. This excludes the last reduction at61 and proves
61 is not in S(15).
